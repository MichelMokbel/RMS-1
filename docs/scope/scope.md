# Scope: Customer identity, payments, and promotions

Extend RMS and the customer ordering website so customers can register with automatic customer matching or creation, pay for orders and memberships, receive the right invoice and payment records, and use membership promotion codes managed in this dashboard. Provider collections need a separate clearing flow for bank payouts, commissions, fees, and adjustments.

Both applications are in scope. The customer website was located at `/Applications/XAMPP/htdocs/laylakitchen`, not the initially supplied `/Documents/XAMPP/htdocs/laylakitchen`. Its checkout, payment return, account status, membership code entry, and returning customer meal selection are part of the same delivery, not a later optional integration.

**Development handoff, 2026-09-01:** [Consolidated plan](../specs/payment-integration-development-plan.md) maps all ten features, implementation order, shared migration and locking responsibilities, customer journeys, verification gates and remaining owner inputs. The owner accepted the final five designs after the independent review and all three corrections. Their design boxes are complete and their ready to build milestones are recorded below. The specifications remain `Proposed` because implementation has not started.

Confirmed financial ownership: the default accounting company in RMS owns SkipCash payments and clearing. Use the existing accounting company default and retain the resolved owner when checkout starts, so retries and later changes to defaults cannot move an existing transaction to another company. Preserve the existing order or subscription branch and validate its accounting company alignment before collecting money. Payments, invoices, allocations, clearing, and the default bank account must agree on the owning company.

Confirmed payout bank: the existing default bank account for the default accounting company receives SkipCash payouts. Use that account when posting the settlement. Do not add a separate SkipCash bank choice. If the default bank account is missing or inactive, keep the settlement unposted and show an RMS setup exception until an admin corrects the existing default.

Confirmed settlement source: the supplied SkipCash XLSX report will be used for settlement imports in this release. Import both sale rows and separate settlement fee rows, reconcile existing payments, and use the reported commission and net amounts rather than assuming a universal fee rate. Customer phone numbers are useful supporting evidence for staff matching, but cannot independently confirm a payment, allocate funds, or link customer accounts.

Confirmed membership rule: for a completed membership purchase with a positive final amount, a discount changes the price only. The purchased meal allowance and normal meal consumption stay unchanged. Each meal still reduces the remaining count normally, including after payment allocation or usage recalculation. A promotion that reduces the final amount to zero follows the separate request only rule below and does not establish an allowance at that point.

Confirmed undiscounted package prices: QAR 900 for 20 meals and QAR 1,200 for 26 meals. Use the package total as the pricing authority, not a rounded daily rate multiplied by the meal count. Both applications must agree on the approved prices, and each purchase must retain its price and discount snapshot so later price changes do not reprice existing memberships or invoices.

Confirmed tax rule: there is no tax treatment in this flow. QAR 900 and QAR 1,200 are the complete membership prices before any promotion. Do not calculate, add, allocate, or show tax on the purchase or its daily invoices. The daily invoice net amounts must still total the package amount after the promotion, with no later tax charge.

Confirmed ordinary order policy for this release: after the website purchase is fully funded by a verified new payment, issue and settle the order invoice and recognize its revenue using the existing invoice posting flow, including orders placed in advance. Do not offer or apply saved customer credit at website checkout. The provider payment records newly collected funds; neither that receipt nor later delivery or bank settlement recognizes the invoice revenue again. A new delivery based revenue recognition workflow is not part of this release. This records the owner's requested application policy, not a conclusion about compliance with reporting standards; finance should confirm its suitability for advance sales before production use.

Confirmed fulfillment definition for this release: treat an order as fulfilled in RMS when its invoice is fully paid through a valid payment allocation. Do not require the single current operator to advance `Draft`, `Confirmed`, `InProduction`, `Ready`, `OutForDelivery`, or `Delivered` states. Do not set `Delivered` automatically from payment because that would falsely claim physical delivery and create misleading history. Order states are not an authoritative source for fulfillment, membership use, cancellation eligibility, or reporting in this release. Payment, invoice, allocation, booking date, and meal balance records are authoritative. Customer pages must still call a future meal booked or scheduled, not delivered, even though its paid daily invoice satisfies the internal fulfillment proxy. A 100 percent discount request creates no invoice and therefore no fulfilled order. Moving to operational fulfillment based on the `Delivered` state is deferred until a larger team actively manages the order workflow. That later change must define transition ownership, pickup treatment, delivery proof, failed delivery, and its effect on finance and meal use before replacing this rule.

Confirmed membership billing: keep the existing payment and daily invoice model when the final amount due is positive. Record one new payment for that amount after any allowed promotion and keep it tied to the purchased allowance block in the customer's queue. Allocate each daily invoice against the payment for the block whose credits supplied that meal. Do not apply previously retained customer credit at membership checkout. This restriction does not remove the normal daily allocation from the new purchase payment. Do not issue an invoice for the full package at purchase or recognize the entire receipt as membership revenue then. Under the existing ledger flow, the unallocated amount remains a customer advance, each daily invoice recognizes its own revenue, and allocation reduces the correct advance and settles the invoice. A promotion that reduces the final amount to zero creates only a meal plan request and never enters this billing flow unless a later authorized workflow completes it.

Confirmed partial meal selection: customers can purchase the full membership while selecting only some of its meals, then return to choose the rest within its booking, status, and quota rules. The initial purchase covers the full package price after any allowed promotion, not just the meals selected that day. Booking additional covered meals uses the customer's existing membership queue without another gateway charge, receipt, membership purchase, request conversion, or promo redemption. This is normal use of an existing entitlement, not customer spending of saved credit, so it does not need an admin credit allocation. An eligible membership bought before this integration must also remain usable after ownership and its existing balances are established.

Confirmed meal quantity and credit rule: meal credits are not tied to calendar days. A customer may book any number of main dishes for the same eligible future service date, or spread them across several eligible dates, as long as the selected membership has enough available credits. In the membership selection flow, the customer selects only main dishes. Each selected main dish quantity reserves one credit and each consumed main dish counts once through the established usage flow. Sides included with that main dish consume no additional credit. For example, booking 6 main dishes with their included sides for tomorrow from a new 20 meal membership leaves 14 available. A date does not add a daily limit, reset the allowance, or make several main dishes count as one. The server must validate the full requested main dish quantity against the latest balance so repeated or concurrent submissions cannot exceed the membership allowance. The no same day online rule still applies to every selected main dish.

Confirmed purchase without initial selections: customers may buy a membership allowance with zero meals selected, including when future menus are not yet published, when the final amount remains positive. Zero selections do not mean a zero price. Collect the full package price after any allowed partial discount through the confirmed payment path. On a first paid purchase, create the subscription and mark its request converted immediately. On a later paid purchase, record and convert the completed request immediately and append its full allowance to the existing queue without another competing active subscription balance. Do not invent meal dates, create placeholder meal orders or daily invoices, or automatically choose meals to complete the paid purchase. Show that no meals are scheduled yet and show the allowance available in sequence for later booking. A later booking uses the queue and the correct original funding without another payment, purchase, or promo redemption. A 100 percent discount creates only the pending meal plan request described below, whether the customer selected zero, some, or all proposed meals.

Confirmed online service date rule: the customer website does not accept meal orders for the current Qatar service date at any time. Online ordering is available for eligible dates from tomorrow onward. This applies to ordinary orders, initial membership selections, and later covered membership bookings. Buying a paid membership with no meal selections remains available because it creates no meal booking. Submitting a 100 percent discount request also creates no meal booking. This replaces the earlier before 10:00 am rule and its payment crossing exception.

Confirmed midnight checkout rule: a server validated payment checkout that began while every held service date was still in the future may finish after midnight within its valid checkout window, even though the earliest held date is now today. For example, checkout created at 11:58 pm for tomorrow and paid at 12:02 am is accepted. This honors an existing unchanged hold; it does not allow a new same day attempt. The qualifying start is the server recorded payment attempt created after date, price, menu, availability, ownership, promo, and quota validation. Opening the page, adding an item to the cart, or supplying a browser timestamp does not qualify. Hold the reviewed dates and scarce entitlement without marking the order paid, activating a paid membership, consuming a meal, or completing promo redemption until payment is verified. An exact retry recovers the same attempt. Any change to dates, meals, membership, quantity, or promo requires new validation and cannot use the exception after midnight. A delayed provider notification does not invalidate payment that completed within the valid window. Release an unsuccessful or expired unpaid hold without creating a booking or consuming a promo use. Ordinary payment after the configured window still completes the original saved order under the revised rule below. The existing membership timing rule is unchanged.

Confirmed configurable checkout window: use 15 minutes as the default payment checkout duration and booking hold. Authorized staff can change this duration from RMS payment settings without deploying code. Keep the setting in the recorded owning company context and prevent unauthorized or cross company changes. Validate a positive, operationally safe value, show the unit clearly, and audit the actor, time, old value, and new value. A setting change applies only to payment attempts created afterward. Each attempt records its own server start, duration, and expiry, so an active checkout is never shortened or extended by a later setting change. The website shows the remaining time using the server expiry rather than the device clock. Payment verified before the recorded expiry may complete the unchanged held purchase, including across midnight. For ordinary orders, the timer limits new initiation and payment link display, not acceptance of an actual verified payment for an existing attempt. If a dispatched provider outcome remains pending or unknown, continue recovery without prompting another charge. An unpaid target may be released but its original snapshot remains available for verified completion once. A fresh or changed cart still requires current date validation. Membership holds retain their existing expiry behavior.

Confirmed ordinary payment after expiry rule, revised 2026-08-30: when the first fully verified matching SkipCash payment arrives for an ordinary checkout, create the original saved order, paid invoice, payment, and allocation even if payment completed exactly at or after the recorded expiry. Keep the confirmed meals, dates, prices, company, and terms. Send the normal order confirmation. Do not turn the payment into unused credit, require a new checkout, or send the customer to support solely because time elapsed. Existing payment verification, finance locks, historical posting dates, and exact retry protection still apply. One attempt creates its original orders once; an actual additional collection remains governed by the duplicate payment rule, not permission to create another order.

Membership payment timing is not changed by this ordinary order revision. A payment completed at or after an expired membership attempt remains a recorded unallocated customer payment under its existing no refund rule, without automatic membership conversion, booking, or promo redemption. Only an authorized admin may later allocate that credit. Its separate membership and promotion specifications remain responsible for that flow.

Confirmed mixed date cart experience: when a cart contains today and future dates, clearly mark today's selections as unavailable online and show the ordering contact message. Exclude today's selections before any payment, booking, meal use, promo completion, invoice, or fulfillment action. Show the revised dates, quantities, and payable total, then let the customer confirm and place the eligible future portion normally. Do not silently drop today's food or charge the original mixed total. For a membership booking, do not reserve or consume quota for today; future covered meals continue without another payment. For a new membership purchase, the full package purchase may still complete under the confirmed price rule, while only eligible future meal selections are booked. If the cart contains only today, create no online meal order and collect no order payment; the customer may still buy a membership with no selections.

Confirmed customer message: “Today's meals cannot be ordered online. To ask about availability, kindly contact us on 55683442. Your other eligible dates can continue.” The approved operational number is `55683442` and must be exposed consistently rather than duplicating conflicting contact details. Contacting the restaurant is a request for help, not a promise that a same day order will be accepted.

Timezone interpretation for this plan: use Qatar local time (`Asia/Qatar`) to determine whether a selected service date is today, consistent with the Doha business default documented in `config/app.php`. This is not a claim about the deployed environment setting, which must be checked before launch. The website and RMS must agree on the service date regardless of the customer's device timezone or clock. Replace the website's existing 24 hour notice wording with the confirmed no same day ordering message; enforce the date rule in RMS as well as the website.

Confirmed membership validity: completed memberships have no time based expiry for this release. Unused meal entitlements remain available regardless of how much time has passed for paid and partially discounted memberships. A customer who buys 20 meals, selects 5, and returns six weeks later still has the other 15 available, subject to existing bookings, consumption, pause, and cancellation rules. The end of a menu month, inactivity, or an estimated end date must not forfeit meals or force another purchase. Preserve purchase and service dates for history and scheduling, not as an expiry countdown. This does not create unlimited meals, restore consumed meals, reactivate a cancelled membership, give an allowance to a pending 100 percent discount request, or remove booking deadlines. Meal availability remains governed by the confirmed quota calculation.

Confirmed customer booking change rule: customers can change or cancel eligible future membership meal bookings through the website before one configured clock time on the calendar day before service in Qatar. The default accounting company owns one company wide cutoff setting for every branch. The default is 11:00 pm in `Asia/Qatar` on the day before service. Authorized staff can change that time in RMS. Every branch inherits the same setting and a branch cannot override it in this release. Unauthorized and cross company changes are denied. Record the actor, time, old value, and new value in the audit history. For example, if service is Wednesday and the setting remains at its default, the online change deadline is Tuesday at 11:00 pm Qatar time for every branch. Each booking records the company cutoff time and timezone when it is confirmed. A later settings change applies only to bookings confirmed afterward and never shortens or extends the deadline promised to an existing booking. If an eligible existing booking moves to another service date, calculate its new deadline from its recorded cutoff time and the new service date in Qatar, not from the later company setting. Enforce the deadline on the server and show the exact Qatar deadline to the customer. This rule does not calculate a rolling number of hours from service and never uses the device clock. Before the recorded cutoff, a change to the service date, main dish, or quantity must revalidate the menu, no same day rule, total available credits, and current booking state. Cancelling a booking or reducing its quantity releases only the unused reserved main dish credits back to the same purchase blocks from which they were reserved, preserving queue order. This is a reservation restoration, not customer credit, a refund, a new payment, or new revenue. If a daily invoice or other posted record already exists, use its established correction workflow and preserve the history. At or after the recorded cutoff, the website makes no automatic change and restores no credits. Keep the booking unchanged, explain that the online deadline passed, and direct the customer to the restaurant contact. Contact does not guarantee that staff can accept the change. Any staff action must use the authorized owning workflow.

Confirmed membership pause rule: when a pause becomes effective, cancel every future membership booking whose service date falls inside the pause period, regardless of its unused order status or whether its daily invoice already satisfies the paid invoice fulfillment proxy. Block new bookings and automatic generation for that period. Cancel a draft daily invoice. Void or reverse an issued or posted daily invoice through the established AR correction workflow, preserving its history and respecting finance locks. Release or reverse the related allocation from the original membership payment through the same workflow. Keep the original payment recorded and return the released amount to the same purchase block so it can fund later daily invoices. Restore each affected main dish credit to the exact purchase block from which it was reserved, preserve queue order, and recalculate booked, used, and available balances. Included sides restore no additional credit. The booking cancellation, invoice correction, allocation release, and meal restoration must succeed together and be safe to repeat. Record the pause, affected records, actor, reason, and before and after values. This is not a refund, a new payment, saved credit allocation, new revenue, or promo restoration. Do not use order state as the pause eligibility check. A service date before the effective pause period is not undone. Once the pause period ends, the restored meals remain available for eligible later dates without payment or membership expiry. A pause uses the authorized owning workflow and is not blocked by the customer website change cutoff, but finance exceptions remain visible for review.

Confirmed invoice driven order cancellation rule: the linked daily invoice is the source of truth. When that invoice is voided through the existing AR workflow, treat its linked subscription order as cancelled and update the payment allocation and invoice based subscription usage through the current logic. Keep the original payment recorded so its released balance can fund a later order from the same membership. Editing the invoice does not cancel the order and does not change this rule. Voiding an edited invoice has the same cancellation result as voiding the original invoice. Do not add a separate service failure decision, kitchen quantity model, membership meal record, cancellation model, or second order cancellation action. Create no refund, new payment, provider event, promo restoration, or general website credit. Preserve current audit history and finance locks, and make repeated invoice void handling safe from a second order cancellation, allocation release, or meal count update.

Confirmed membership visibility: after sign in, the website shows the customer's membership balance before asking them to buy another plan. It shows the active allowance, any later purchased allowance waiting in sequence, meals already used, meals already selected or booked, and how many more can be chosen, with the booked dates and fulfillment status available to inspect. The customer can choose covered meals from this balance or deliberately buy another allowance to add to the end of the queue. The customer does not choose which purchase supplies a booking. A missing menu, exhausted total quota, or an ineligible date must not silently send the customer into a new paid purchase.

Membership availability must account for both used meals and saved bookings across the ordered purchase queue. Count each entitlement once: available to choose equals the total fully purchased allowance in the queue minus used meals minus bookings not already counted as used. For example, one 20 meal purchase with 2 used and 3 other booked meals has 15 available. If another 20 meal purchase was completed under the same customer, the total available becomes 35, while deductions still use the first purchase before the second. Preserve the existing invoice based consumption event; reserving a meal and later consuming it cannot deduct two meals. For this release, a fully paid daily invoice is the internal fulfillment proxy and the meal use event. Customer wording remains based on the service date, so an invoiced future meal is shown as booked or scheduled and is never described as physically delivered. Counts come from the server and remain correct across visits, devices, concurrent submissions, staff changes, and scheduled jobs, not from the browser cart alone or unused order states.

Confirmed sequential membership consumption: a customer may buy another membership allowance before the current one is exhausted, including to cover meals ordered for themselves and a family member under the same customer. The new purchase is paid and recorded immediately, but its allowance is added after the current allowance instead of becoming a second selectable active membership. Every booking consumes the oldest fully funded allowance with remaining credits. When that allowance reaches zero, consumption moves automatically to the next completed purchase. If one booking crosses the boundary, use the remaining credits from the first purchase, then the required credits from the next. The customer never chooses a funding source and the system must not operate two competing active subscription balances for the same queue. Each purchase still keeps its own price, discount, payment, promotion, company, branch, currency, and audit history so daily invoices draw from the correct funding as the queue advances. A repeat purchase does not remove unused credits or cause an automatic recurring charge.

Confirmed family order details: every membership meal under one customer uses that customer's name, phone number, and delivery address. Meals intended for a family member are additional main dish quantities under the same customer and consume the same ordered membership queue. Do not add separate recipient names, addresses, contact details, or saved recipient profiles in this release. Each confirmed booking keeps the customer and delivery details used when it was placed, so a later profile change applies to new bookings and does not silently rewrite existing ones.

Confirmed checkout separation: keep one membership purchase in its own checkout. Do not combine it with an ordinary order or separately priced extras. Included salad and dessert remain part of each selected main dish and are not paid extras. The customer website already uses one plan mode or flexible order mode for the whole checkout. In membership mode it sets the included side quantities from the selected main dish quantity and disables separate side quantity controls. Preserve this behavior. Any ordinary item or paid extra uses a separate checkout and its own payment and invoice flow. RMS must enforce the same boundary from server calculated facts and reject a changed request that mixes purchase types or asks for extra included items. The current RMS request validation accepts independent side quantities and therefore does not yet prove this server rule. Closing that gap belongs to the planned membership checkout work.

Confirmed membership delivery price: delivery is included in the QAR 900 price for 20 meals and the QAR 1,200 price for 26 meals. Do not add a delivery fee at membership purchase, initial meal selection, or any later covered booking. Delivery creates no separate payment, invoice line, customer balance, meal deduction, or provider collection. A change in the number of service dates does not change the package price. Ordinary orders and separately paid extras keep their own pricing rules. Keep existing order address fields as they work today. Do not add delivery area validation, address eligibility checks, delivery availability rules, delivery settings, or a new delivery flow.

Later meal bookings retain the price and discount snapshot of the completed purchase block whose credit they consume. A paid purchase block's daily invoice is not made free just because there is no new checkout payment. It draws from that block's original payment through the existing allocation flow. A 100 percent discount request supplies no credits and cannot fund a daily invoice. Preserve each completed block's recorded financial owner, branch, and currency, including for legacy records. A later change to the gateway default company cannot move its balance. When one booking crosses from one block to the next, keep the consumed quantities and invoice funding attributable to the correct purchase blocks. Across all selection visits, invoice values and rounding still reconcile to each original package, and later promo expiry or deactivation does not remove a partial discount already purchased. Meal bookings create no new collection, provider fee, or clearing event.

Confirmed refund boundary: refunds are not included in this release. Any unused paid balance remains customer credit backed by the original payment, not a second payment or an additional credit balance. Cancelling a membership does not void its payment, restore amounts already consumed, or turn promotional discounts into credit. Existing invoice and allocation history remains auditable.

Confirmed credit allocation authority: only an admin in RMS may allocate saved customer credit for now. Customers cannot use it at any website checkout, including ordinary orders, membership purchases, and renewals. This supersedes earlier approval of customer credit use at checkout. Enforce the restriction on the server, not just by hiding a payment option, and do not let invoice creation or recovery jobs silently apply old balances. Normal automatic allocation of daily invoices against the payment already linked to that membership remains unchanged.

Keep saved credit and its original payment history intact. Admin allocation uses the canonical AR workflow with customer, company, branch, currency, available balance, period lock, and audit safeguards. Support or finance access alone does not authorize it. Allocating saved credit creates no new receipt, provider collection, clearing entry, or additional revenue. Funds already allocated or committed to another purchase or active membership cannot be spent again, and historical or unrelated invoices must not count as meals in a new membership.

Confirmed credit validity: unused paid customer credit never expires, including after membership cancellation or a legacy record marked expired. A historical expiry label and the age of a payment must not forfeit this credit or turn it into revenue. The separate no expiry membership rule protects unused meals; neither rule restores consumed meals, reactivates cancelled memberships, or creates credit from a promotional discount.

Confirmed membership promotion allocation: when a partial discount leaves a positive final amount, spread that purchase discount across the daily invoices. Each invoice retains its gross price, allocated discount, and net amount. The net amounts for the full meal allowance total the discounted package price and equal the new membership payment. Handle rounding remainders explicitly so the final allocation leaves no artificial unpaid balance or unused payment caused by rounding. The partial discount does not change the number of meals consumed. A 100 percent discount creates no purchase block or daily invoices at request submission.

Confirmed promotion eligibility: staff can configure each code for first membership purchases only, renewals only, or both. A first purchase means the customer's first completed membership purchase, not the first purchase of a particular package or the first payment through this gateway. Earlier completed memberships still count after expiry, including any historically funded from credit. This does not enable credit at new membership checkout. A pending 100 percent discount request is not a completed membership purchase and does not by itself change later first purchase or renewal eligibility. Codes restricted to renewals also cover a repeat paid purchase added to the customer's membership queue, such as a parent buying more allowance for orders under their name. Eligibility follows the purchasing customer's completed history. Ordinary orders alone do not count as membership purchases, and pending, failed, or abandoned checkouts do not count as completed purchases. The example code names and 10 percent discounts discussed are illustrative, not codes to create automatically.

Confirmed promotion limit per purchase: a membership purchase may use at most one promo code. Combining multiple codes is not allowed. If the final amount due is positive, the customer pays it through SkipCash without using retained customer credit. Promo codes may cover the full membership price, but cannot create a negative amount due or customer credit from any excess discount.

Confirmed promotion administration and sharing: only an admin in RMS generates and manages membership promo codes. The admin shares each code manually with customers outside RMS. Do not add automatic distribution, customer lists for sending codes, or a new messaging provider to this feature.

Confirmed promotion configuration: an admin may create either a percentage discount or a fixed QAR discount. The discount cannot exceed the selected package price or create customer credit. Every code requires a start date, end date, and positive total redemption limit. The admin sets a per customer redemption limit, which defaults to one. A 100 percent code is always locked to one redemption per customer. The admin selects whether the code applies to the 20 meal plan, the 26 meal plan, or both, and separately selects first purchases, renewals, or both under the confirmed eligibility rule. A code is available company wide across every branch for any eligible customer who receives it manually. Do not add recipient assignment or branch specific promo rules.

Confirmed promotion cancellation rule: once a paid or partially discounted membership purchase is completed, its allowance is established in the queue, and its request is converted, cancelling it does not restore the redeemed promo code use, even if no meals have been used yet. Preserve the redemption history and keep that use counted against the code's total and customer usage limits. Cancellation does not restore first purchase eligibility. The code itself remains available for other eligible uses within its configured rules, rather than being disabled by one cancellation. An unsuccessful paid checkout is not a completed redemption. A valid 100 percent discount request follows its separate once per customer redemption rule below.

Confirmed 100 percent discount request policy: when a valid promotion reduces the server calculated final membership amount to exactly zero, create only one pending meal plan request. Every 100 percent promotion is redeemable once per customer. The first valid request submission counts as that customer's redemption. The same customer cannot use the same code to create another request. Exact retries and later submissions return the existing request. Rejection or cancellation does not grant another redemption. Do not create or link a subscription, purchase block, queue allowance, order, booking, daily invoice, payment, payment link, allocation, receipt, customer advance, provider event, fee, clearing entry, revenue, meal use, or converted request. Do not contact SkipCash or allocate saved credit. The request itself retains the customer, plan, proposed meal choices, code, eligibility result, price, full discount, company, branch, currency, redemption, and submission audit details needed for staff review. Show the customer that the request was submitted and that no active membership or meals were created yet. A browser supplied zero price is never sufficient. Staff review and manually convert the request later through the existing RMS meal plan request process. This feature adds no separate approval process and does not automatically create anything after submission.

Confirmed automatic conversion: when a first membership has a positive amount due, once it is fully covered by a verified new payment, record that payment, create and link the subscription, and mark the associated meal plan request as `converted` without waiting for staff approval. A later paid purchase under the same customer records its payment and conversion immediately, then extends the ordered allowance queue instead of opening another competing active subscription balance. A 100 percent discount request remains pending and does not use this automatic conversion path. Do not draw on previously retained customer credit in any case. Preserve each completed purchase's chosen service context and normal meal use. Conversion itself is not a meal. Background checks follow the successful paid flow and raise exceptions, but do not replace transaction validation, ownership checks, or protection against duplicate payment, redemption, allowance, and subscription effects.

Confirmed customer experience: uncertainty about an existing customer match must never block registration, ordering, payment, or automatic membership conversion. When a safe existing link cannot be established promptly, create a separate customer record owned by the registering account and continue the normal flow. Any provisional or review flag is internal to staff, not a customer approval gate. The customer can access their new activity but not another candidate customer's history; staff review the potential link afterward. Normal authentication, payment verification, and order validation still apply independently of matching confidence.

Confirmed duplicate customer resolution: use the existing RMS customer merge flow when review confirms that a new website customer and an older customer record belong to the same person. Do not add a separate missing membership recovery flow. Registration, ordering, and payment remain available while the possible duplicate awaits admin review. Before this integration ships, extend the existing merge flow so one transaction moves every current and newly added customer owned reference to the surviving customer. This includes orders, subscriptions, purchase blocks, meal reservations and usage, invoices, payments and allocations, payment attempts and provider records, sales, pastry orders, meal plan requests, promotion redemptions, phone verification records, quotations, order sheet entries, portal ownership, and related review exceptions. Preserve historical order and invoice snapshots rather than rewriting them. The merge must keep one usable portal account, preserve each financial record and funding link, combine membership allowances in their established sequence, preserve promotion usage, deactivate the source customer, record the actor and result, and be safe to repeat without duplicating money, meals, bookings, or promo uses.

Confirmed promotion behavior after a customer merge: honor every discount completed before the merge and never claw it back. Combine the source and target membership purchase and promo redemption history during the existing merge flow. Evaluate every later promo attempt against that combined history and the code limits. A merge cannot restore a redemption, recreate first purchase eligibility, or let the merged customer exceed either the per customer limit or the total code limit.

Confirmed customer communication: the website presents or links the current Terms and Conditions before the customer confirms. Those terms explain membership validity, booking deadlines, no refunds, and admin only credit use. Send confirmations by email for completed payments, paid membership purchases, covered bookings, and submitted 100 percent discount requests. An email failure does not change the recorded result and must not cause another payment, booking, request, or redemption.

**Build approach:** Tracer Bullet (prove one narrow real payment through every layer, then add breadth).
**Workflow:** GA (`/check verify`, `/test`, a fresh `/check review`, then `/document`). The project default level of rigor. `/architect` is the recommended first stop for a feature with a real decision, but skippable when you already know the build. Any feature can carry its own tag to do more or less.

_These are recommendations to keep your build orderly, not requirements. Skip anything that does not fit. You decide when a feature is `done`._

The journeys below provide acceptance requirements for recovery and usability within the same features, not new implementation tasks. This is planned coverage, not a claim that the new screens exist or that these journeys have passed tests.

## Deferred

- **Customer use of saved credit at checkout**: no website payment option or automatic application of saved balances for orders or memberships. Only admins in RMS can allocate saved credit; normal daily allocation from a membership's own payment continues.
- **Refunds**: no full or partial refund integration, refund initiation endpoint, or customer refund action in this release. Keep unused paid funds as customer credit. Any unexpected provider refund or reversal is recorded for finance review rather than ignored or automatically posted.
- **Automatic payout retrieval**: add only if confirmed SkipCash APIs provide reliable settlement and fee data. The first release uses the supplied XLSX report format through a reviewed import flow.
- **Dispute automation**: keep chargeback ingestion and provider dispute workflows out of the first slice unless SkipCash operations require them for launch.
- **Delivered state fulfillment**: do not require routine order state changes in this release. When the operations team grows, design a managed kitchen and delivery workflow that replaces the paid invoice fulfillment proxy with the `Delivered` state and defines pickup, proof, failed service, finance effects, and meal use.
